BIRD'S-MEDIA


Eagle

Eagles are among the most fascinating and powerful birds on Earth. Let me give you some key details and specialities about them:
🔹 General Details: Type: Large bird of prey (raptor)
Family: Accipitridae (same as hawks, kites, and vultures)
Habitat: Found almost everywhere except Antarctica. Common in forests, mountains, grasslands, and near rivers or coasts.
Diet: Carnivorous – mainly fish, small mammals, reptiles, and other birds.
Symbolism: Often seen as a symbol of power, strength, courage, and freedom.

Eagle's Sound

🧠 Fun Facts about Eagle Sounds:
They use sound mainly for communication – with mates, chicks, or to warn intruders.
They are silent hunters – unlike crows or parrots, they don’t call often while hunting.
Baby eagles (eaglets) make high-pitched begging calls to get food.

🔹 Specialities of Eagles:
Excellent Vision 👀
Eagles can see 4–5 times farther than humans.
They can spot prey from up to 3 kilometers (2 miles) away.
Their eyes are large and take up almost half the size of their head.
Powerful Flight 🕊️
They can soar at heights of 10,000 feet (3 km).
Speed: Some eagles can dive at 320 km/h (200 mph).
They use air currents to glide with minimal effort.
Strong Talons & Beak 🦅
Talons are extremely sharp and strong, capable of crushing bones.
Their grip is 10 times stronger than a human hand.
The hooked beak tears flesh easily.
Longevity 🕰️
Wild eagles can live 20–30 years, sometimes more in captivity.

Peacock

About the Peacock:
Species: The male of the Indian Peafowl (scientific name: Pavo cristatus).
Famous for: Its huge, colorful tail feathers (train) with “eye” patterns, used in courtship displays. Symbolism: Beauty, pride, royalty, and immortality in many cultures. Habitat: Native to India and Sri Lanka, but found in many parts of the world.

peacock Sound

Peacock Sound:
Male Call: Loud, sharp, high-pitched cry that sounds like “may-awe, may-awe” or “mee-ow”. Some people compare it to a cat’s meow but louder and sharper. Calls become frequent during monsoon season (breeding time). Female (Peahen) Call: Softer and less frequent. Used mostly for communication with chicks or when alarmed. Chicks (Peachicks): Make high-pitched “peep-peep” sounds.

peacock 🧠 Fun Facts: A male peacock often calls to attract females or warn of danger. Their calls can travel long distances (up to 1 mile in forests). They are very noisy birds, especially in groups.

Pelican

🔹 About Pelicans:
Family: Pelecanidae Special Feature: A large throat pouch under their long bill, which they use to scoop up fish. Size: Very large water birds, with wingspans up to 3.5 meters (11 feet) in some species. Habitat: Found near lakes, rivers, coasts, and wetlands across most of the world. Diet: Mostly fish, sometimes amphibians or small crustaceans. Behavior: They are excellent fliers despite their size and often fly in V-formations like geese.

pelican sound

Pelican Sound:
Unlike eagles or peacocks, pelicans are usually quiet birds. General Call: Low, guttural grunts, croaks, or growls. Chicks: Begging calls that sound like squealing or squawking. During Mating Season: Adults may produce deep croaking sounds to communicate. 👉 Fun fact: Pelicans do not have a true voice box (syrinx) like songbirds, so their calls are raspy and rough, not musical.

Fun Facts about Pelicans: Group Feeding: Sometimes they form a circle and drive fish into shallow water to scoop them up together. Ancient Birds: Fossils show pelicans existed 30 million years ago. Largest Beak: The Australian Pelican has the world’s longest beak—over 15 inches (40 cm).

grey peacock

About the Grey Peacock-Pheasant: Appearance: Plumage is grey-brown with beautiful eye-like spots on wings and tail. The “eyes” on its feathers look similar to a peacock’s, which is why it’s called a peacock-pheasant. Size: Medium-sized bird, about 50–75 cm in length. Habitat: Found in northeastern India, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, and surrounding forests. Behavior: Shy and secretive, lives in dense forests. Male fans out its tail and wings (like a peacock) during courtship displays.

Grey peacock Sound

🔊 Grey Peacock-Pheasant Sound: Their calls are low, booming “whoo-hoo” or “hoo-oo” notes, repeated slowly. Sometimes make soft whistles and clucking sounds. The call is usually heard in early mornings and evenings. Males call more often in the breeding season to attract females.

🧠 Fun Facts:

It is the state bird of Myanmar. Unlike the Indian peacock, it doesn’t have bright blue or green feathers—its beauty is in the patterned spots. It prefers walking on the ground and only flies short distances when disturbed.

shoebil

🔹 About the Shoebill: Name: Shoebill (also called Whalehead or Shoe-billed Stork). Appearance: Huge bird (about 4–5 feet tall) with a massive, shoe-shaped beak. Grey plumage, long legs, piercing yellow eyes. Habitat: Freshwater swamps and marshes in East-Central Africa (Uganda, Zambia, Sudan). Diet: Carnivorous – eats fish (especially lungfish), baby crocodiles, frogs, snakes, and even turtles. Behavior: Known for being silent and still for long periods while hunting. Very calm and patient bird.

shoebil sound

🔊 Shoebill Sound: Shoebills are mostly silent, but when they do vocalize, their sound is unique: Bill-Clattering 🪵: They clap their huge beaks together rapidly, making a sound like a machine gun or wooden clapping. Often done as a greeting or during nesting. Mooing / Groaning 🐄: Sometimes they produce deep mooing or croaking sounds, especially young ones begging for food. Low Whines or Hiccups: Rare, soft sounds used for communication.

🧠 Fun Facts: Shoebills are called “statue birds” because they stand motionless for hours waiting for prey. Despite their size, they can fly, though slowly and heavily. They are considered one of the most prehistoric-looking birds alive today.

lyre bird

🔹 About the Lyrebird: Species: Superb Lyrebird (Menura novaehollandiae) Albert’s Lyrebird (Menura alberti) Appearance: Medium–large bird native to Australia. Males have long, fancy tail feathers shaped like a lyre (a musical instrument). Habitat: Rainforests and eucalyptus forests of Australia. Diet: Insects, worms, and small invertebrates.

lyre bird sound

Lyrebird Sound: This is what makes it legendary! Lyrebirds are masters of mimicry — they can copy almost any sound they hear. They can imitate: Other bird calls (like kookaburras, parrots, cockatoos). Human-made sounds such as: Chainsaws Car alarms Camera shutters Dog barks Mobile ringtones Males use this talent during courtship displays, singing with tail feathers spread to impress females.

🧠 Fun Facts: A lyrebird can remember and repeat over 20–30 different sounds in one performance. Their syrinx (voice organ) is the most complex among songbirds. Some lyrebirds have been recorded copying sounds they heard years earlier. David Attenborough’s famous documentary showed a lyrebird perfectly imitating a chainsaw cutting wood!

crow

About Crows: Family: Corvidae (includes ravens, magpies, rooks). Species: Many types — House Crow, Jungle Crow, American Crow, Carrion Crow, etc. Habitat: Found worldwide (cities, villages, forests, farmlands). Intelligence: Crows are one of the smartest birds — they can use tools, recognize faces, and even solve puzzles.

crow sound

🔊 Crow Sound: The most common call: “Caw, caw, caw” — a harsh, loud, and raspy sound. But crows don’t just say caw! They have a variety of calls: Alarm calls: Sharp “caw-caw-caw-caw” in quick bursts to warn others. Gathering calls: Long, drawn-out “caaaw” to call other crows. Young crows: High-pitched begging cries like “waaah, waaah”. Communication calls: Soft clicks, rattles, or coo-like sounds when talking to each other.

👉 Fun fact: Some crows can also mimic human speech if trained, though not as clearly as parrots. 🧠 Fun Facts about Crow Sounds: Each crow has its own voice, and other crows can recognize individuals by sound. They have a language-like system with over 20 different calls. Crows often make louder sounds at dawn and dusk, when they gather in groups called roosts.
